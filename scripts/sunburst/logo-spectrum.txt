Logo design exploration for "Clearline", an independent, non-profit drug checking lab that tests street drug samples with reagent kits, FTIR spectroscopy and fentanyl test strips, and publishes the results for harm reduction. The brand must feel calm, trustworthy, scientific but human, like a public health service people can rely on. Dark mode first.

Present a clean brand sheet on a flat near-black navy background (#0a0c11): the symbol large in the center, and below it a horizontal lockup of the symbol beside the wordmark "Clearline" spelled exactly C-l-e-a-r-l-i-n-e. Also show the symbol small at favicon size in the bottom corner. Palette: soft lavender #a99dff as the single accent, warm off-white #f0efec, nothing else. Flat vector logo, geometric, crisp edges, generous negative space, no gradients, no shadows, no 3D, no mockup props, no extra text, no taglines.

Absolutely no syringes, needles, pills, capsules, powder, smoke, leaves, skulls, crossbones, warning signs, or anything that depicts or glamorizes drug use.

Direction: A spectrum line. A single continuous stroke that starts as a gentle spectroscopy peak (one or two soft peaks like an FTIR readout) and then settles into a perfectly flat, straight line. Reads as "noise resolved into clarity". Monoline, rounded caps. The wordmark is a quiet geometric sans-serif in off-white.
